# Educational videos

[Open the video library](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#videos)

21 public lectures across the three exam subjects. Each resource retains its official source and viewing URL. Topic lectures and background support are labeled separately; none is claimed to replace every proof in the assigned books or papers. Watching earns no cold-exam credit. Source links checked 2026-11-06.

Use the live website for in-page playback. If a provider restricts its player, use the direct viewing link or official lecture page.

## Approximation algorithms

### A Second Course in Algorithms (Lecture 15: Introduction to Approximation Algorithms)

Tim Roughgarden · Stanford University · foundation · background support

Overview of approximation guarantees, makespan scheduling, a brief knapsack scaling summary, and the terminal-MST approximation for Steiner tree. Preparation for the assigned FPTAS, configuration PTAS and Steiner-forest proofs; it does not supply those complete proofs. The instructor's course page links this video; its associated Lecture 15 notes confirm the topic scope.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OacQnsvauZs) · [Official source](https://timroughgarden.org/w16/w16.html) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-approx-OacQnsvauZs)

Topics: Knapsack: an FPTAS with a full bit-complexity argument; Scheduling schemes: large jobs, rounded types, configurations; Generalized Steiner tree: moat growth and the forest lemma.

### A Second Course in Algorithms (Lecture 17: Linear Programming and Approximation Algorithms)

Tim Roughgarden · Stanford University · advanced · direct support

Set-cover LP duality and dual fitting, followed by LP rounding and a primal-dual algorithm for weighted vertex cover. Directly illustrates tight-set charging in the frequency-two special case; the general frequency-f proof remains an extension. The instructor's linked Lecture 17 notes contain the primal-dual algorithm and its full charging argument.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCsnLNZaoPQ) · [Official source](https://timroughgarden.org/w16/w16.html) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-approx-bCsnLNZaoPQ)

Topics: Primal–dual set cover: tight sets and frequency.

### A Second Course in Algorithms (Lecture 18: Five Essential Tools for Analyzing Randomized Algorithms)

Tim Roughgarden · Stanford University · advanced · direct support

Fair-assignment MAX-3SAT, expectation and tail inequalities, Chernoff bounds, and independent path rounding for low-congestion routing. The associated Lecture 18 notes derive logarithmic-over-loglog congestion for unit capacities and demands. It does not cover the MAX-SAT LP's 3/4 guarantee or arbitrary-demand routing without additional hypotheses.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bfxz88uupx0) · [Official source](https://timroughgarden.org/w16/w16.html) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-approx-bfxz88uupx0)

Topics: Random and biased assignments; derandomization; Chernoff bounds and randomized unsplittable routing.

### A Second Course in Algorithms (Lecture 20: Semidefinite Programming and the Maximum Cut Problem)

Tim Roughgarden · Stanford University · advanced · direct support

Derives the vector and Gram-matrix MAX-CUT relaxation, PSD feasibility, random-hyperplane rounding, angular separation probability and the approximately 0.878 approximation. The instructor's linked Lecture 20 notes substantiate this scope. Vector-coloring semicolorings and positive-threshold Gaussian independent-set extraction are separate topics.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8IwoS1io5BM) · [Official source](https://timroughgarden.org/w16/w16.html) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-approx-8IwoS1io5BM)

Topics: Gram matrices and the exact MAX-CUT ratio.

### 17. Complexity: Approximation Algorithms

Srinivas Devadas · Massachusetts Institute of Technology — OpenCourseWare · foundation · background support

Introductory approximation ratios and PTAS/FPTAS distinctions, matching-based vertex cover, greedy set cover, and a two-way partition PTAS. The official transcript confirms these examples. Useful preparation for general identical-machine schemes and primal-dual cover, but not the assigned configuration or general-frequency proofs.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MEz1J9wY2iM) · [Official source](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-046j-design-and-analysis-of-algorithms-spring-2015/resources/lecture-17-complexity-approximation-algorithms/) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-approx-MEz1J9wY2iM)

Topics: Scheduling schemes: large jobs, rounded types, configurations; Primal–dual set cover: tight sets and frequency.

### Approximation schemes in planar graphs: Branchwidth and Baker's technique.

Shay Mozes · Massachusetts Institute of Technology · advanced · background support

MIT 6.889 Lecture 8 develops carving/branch decompositions, a planar radius-to-width bound, and Baker's technique through minimum vertex cover. It supports the structural idea behind planar MIS, but uses a different objective and decomposition presentation from the assigned proof.

[Watch lecture](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.889/fall11/lectures/video/L08_720p.mp4) · [Official source](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.889/fall11/lectures/L08.html) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-approx-approximation-schemes-in-planar-graphs-branchwidth-and-baker-s-technique)

Topics: Baker’s PTAS: layers, treewidth, independent-set DP.


## Interactive proofs and zero knowledge

### 2020.08.27 - IP#1 - Graph Non-Isomorphism & PSPACE Upper Bound

Alessandro Chiesa · University of California, Berkeley · foundation · background support

Introduces interactive proofs, basic GNI and the PSPACE upper bound. Use alongside the public-transcript consistency argument and GMW's additional malicious-verifier certification; those refinements require separate study.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6MshCKY5bTc) · [Official source](https://ic-people.epfl.ch/~achiesa/classes/CS294-F2020.html) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-complexity-6MshCKY5bTc)

Topics: IP ⊆ PSPACE: private information changes the recurrence; Graph nonisomorphism: hidden choices and verifier certification.

### Instance Checking and the Permanent: Graduate Complexity Lecture 16 at CMU

Ryan O'Donnell · Carnegie Mellon University · advanced · background support

Graduate treatment of permanent self-reductions and instance checking. Provides algebraic and program-checking context for LFKN; the assigned paper's complete compression protocol and consequences remain separate reading.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ShI2dOpg7kA) · [Official source](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~odonnell/complexity17/) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-complexity-ShI2dOpg7kA)

Topics: LFKN: verify the permanent, not just a Boolean sum; LFKN: P^#P, PH, program checking and relativization.

### Lecture 1: Interactive Proofs and the Sum-Check Protocol, Part 1

Yael Tauman Kalai · Massachusetts Institute of Technology · advanced · direct support

MIT graduate lecture on interactive proofs and sum-check, with #SAT listed in the official lecture description. Supports algebraic counting verification; supplement it with LFKN's permanent-specific reduction and closure arguments.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iqSMRO78UD0) · [Official source](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-5630-advanced-topics-in-cryptography-fall-2023/resources/65630-f23-lecture-1-part-1_mp4/) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-complexity-iqSMRO78UD0)

Topics: LFKN: P^#P, PH, program checking and relativization.

### 2020.09.03 - IP#3 - IP=PSPACE

Alessandro Chiesa · University of California, Berkeley · advanced · direct support

Covers QBF arithmetization and PSPACE contained in IP. The syllabus explicitly identifies Shamir's protocol with Shen's degree reduction, matching the guide's modern operator presentation.

### [CSS.203.1] Computational Complexity - Lecture 28

Prahladh Harsha · Tata Institute of Fundamental Research · foundation · direct support

Introduces the multiprover model, fixed answer tables, MIP=NEXP and the PCP connection. The accompanying Lecture 28 notes confirm the instructor and topics; this is an overview, not the full BFL multilinearity/decoding proof.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y7skqTcTjCo) · [Official source](https://www.tcs.tifr.res.in/~prahladh/teaching/2020-21/complexity/) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-complexity-Y7skqTcTjCo)

### 2020.09.17 - IP#7 - Zero Knowledge Interactive Proofs

Alessandro Chiesa · University of California, Berkeley · advanced · direct support

Covers GI, honest- and malicious-verifier zero knowledge, and perfect/statistical/computational simulation notions. Supports the definitions and GI unit; the GMW NP construction and GNI certification need their own proofs.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IEZmhLuup0o) · [Official source](https://ic-people.epfl.ch/~achiesa/classes/CS294-F2020.html) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-complexity-IEZmhLuup0o)

Topics: Zero knowledge: malicious views, simulators and quantifiers.

## Probabilistic method

### Bounded Differences Inequality (aka Azuma-Hoeffding Inequality)

Yufei Zhao · Massachusetts Institute of Technology · advanced · direct support

MIT 18.226 short lecture on bounded differences, with coupon-collector and random-graph chromatic-number applications. Direct support for the basic concentration mechanism; it does not replace the assigned sparse four-value chromatic-window argument.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FUwr_EtcPIU) · [Official source](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-226-probabilistic-methods-in-combinatorics-fall-2022/resources/18226-bounded-differences-inequality_mp4/) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-probability-FUwr_EtcPIU)

Topics: Azuma: conditional MGFs and exposure couplings; Four concentration applications: find the right Lipschitz constant; Chromatic concentration: packing and sparse windows.

### Lecture 23: Martingales (Plain, Sub, and Super)

Robert Gallager · Massachusetts Institute of Technology · foundation · background support

MIT 6.262 lecture introducing martingales, submartingales, supermartingales and stopped processes after Wald's identity. Builds the conditional-expectation vocabulary; the official description does not promise the assigned Azuma or adaptive-variance proofs.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GwVjWQykCDw) · [Official source](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-262-discrete-stochastic-processes-spring-2011/resources/lecture-23-martingales-plain-sub-and-super/) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-probability-GwVjWQykCDw)

Topics: Azuma: conditional MGFs and exposure couplings; Gradations and adaptive-query variance accounting.

### Information Theory in Combinatorics I

Shachar Lovett · University of California, San Diego; Simons Institute, University of California, Berkeley (host) · advanced · direct support

Information Theory Boot Camp, January 2015. The official I–II series abstract and shared notes cover entropy, conditioning, chain rules, Shearer's lemma and combinatorial counting. Session I supplies the introduction; not every advertised I–II application is claimed to occur in this one recording.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eCs870ZS7Zk) · [Official source](https://simons.berkeley.edu/talks/information-theory-combinatorics-i) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-probability-eCs870ZS7Zk)

Topics: Entropy: chain rule, conditioning and extremal families; Shearer and Loomis–Whitney: control every coordinate multiplicity.

### Polar Codes I

Erdal Arıkan · Bilkent University; Simons Institute, University of California, Berkeley (host) · advanced · background support

Graduate tutorial on information polarization, polar-code construction and decoding. Useful constructive channel-coding context for Shannon's theorem, rather than the book's random-coding proof. The official abstract assumes basic information theory and elementary martingales.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PNBFUV-ZetY) · [Official source](https://simons.berkeley.edu/talks/polar-codes-i) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-probability-PNBFUV-ZetY)

Topics: Shannon coding: random errors versus worst-case errors.

### Correlation inequalities for Schur functions and Young tableaux

Igor Pak · University of California, Los Angeles; Institute for Pure and Applied Mathematics (IPAM) · advanced · background support

IPAM workshop lecture recorded April 17, 2024, listed by the institutes' official video archive and published on IPAM's channel. Research overview of FKG-type correlation inequalities and poset/algebraic applications; not a substitute for the assigned Four Functions or XYZ proofs.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kQsQ4f2PxAE) · [Official source](https://www.mathinstitutes.org/videos/21560) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-probability-kQsQ4f2PxAE)

Topics: FKG: measure hypotheses and negative examples; Linear extensions and the XYZ correlation theorem.

### Lecture 7: Szemerédi’s Graph Regularity Lemma II: Triangle Removal Lemma

Yufei Zhao · Massachusetts Institute of Technology · advanced · direct support

Triangle counting, the partition–clean–count method, triangle removal and the graph-theoretic proof of Roth's theorem. MIT's current course page is labeled Fall 2023, but its video gallery explicitly says these lectures were recorded for 18.217 in 2019.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RD9AWDdj-Yk) · [Official source](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-225-graph-theory-and-additive-combinatorics-fall-2023/resources/lecture-7-szemeredi2019s-graph-regularity-lemma-ii-triangle-removal-lemma/) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-probability-RD9AWDdj-Yk)

Topics: Triangle removal: a structural lemma becomes a tester.

### Lecture 9: Szemerédi’s Graph Regularity Lemma IV: Induced Removal Lemma

Yufei Zhao · Massachusetts Institute of Technology · advanced · direct support

The official description covers stronger graph regularity, induced removal and graph property testing. Direct support for the structural input in Chapter 17; the exact 3-color restriction tree and semi-hereditary characterization are not asserted here. Recording is from 2019 despite the current Fall 2023 course label.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3IxWLibV_tU) · [Official source](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-225-graph-theory-and-additive-combinatorics-fall-2023/resources/lecture-9-szemeredi2019s-graph-regularity-lemma-iv-induced-removal-lemma/) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-probability-3IxWLibV_tU)

Topics: Induced removal, hereditary properties and oblivious testing; Dense-graph testing and the 3-coloring restriction tree.

### Property Testing for Graphs

Artur Czumaj · University of Warwick; Simons Institute, University of California, Berkeley (host) · advanced · background support

Sublinear Algorithms Boot Camp lecture, May 21, 2024. A graph-testing survey to orient the Chapter 17 material; the official page does not identify it as a proof of Alon–Spencer's particular colorability tester, so that precise coverage is not claimed.

[Watch lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JzYGN28dv_Q) · [Official source](https://simons.berkeley.edu/talks/artur-czumaj-university-warwick-2024-05-21) · [Open player](https://hadi1373z.github.io/doctoral-exam/#video/video-probability-JzYGN28dv_Q)

Topics: Dense-graph testing and the 3-coloring restriction tree; Triangle removal: a structural lemma becomes a tester; Induced removal, hereditary properties and oblivious testing.
